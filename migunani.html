Migunani

Two Javanese sayings have lived rent-free in my head: urip iku urup—a life should bring light to others—and ngelmu iku kalakone kanthi laku—knowledge has to be lived, not just learned. I’ve always wanted to be part of work that matters to other people. I also want to do that work well, and see how far I can take it.

That has taken me through community work, research, and policy, often alongside people who have taught me a great deal. This page brings those experiences together with the opportunities and recognition I’ve received. I’m proud of what I’ve done so far, grateful for who made it possible, and keen to put what I’ve learned to use in bigger ways.

The story behind the highlights

01 · High school

Starting with community work

I was a natural science student in high school, but much of my time outside class went into organisations, volunteering, and youth programmes. Through Mubaligh Hijrah, I was placed in a rural village in Java and worked with local leaders to help organise activities for the community. I also took part in student initiatives that encouraged other young people to volunteer.

Those experiences were often small and practical. They taught me that contributing did not always mean starting something myself. Sometimes it meant joining a team, listening to people who knew the place better than I did, and taking responsibility for the part I could do.

02 · UMY and after

Learning to build with others

At Universitas Muhammadiyah Yogyakarta, I moved between student government, advocacy, external relations, and event organising. I helped put together public discussions, seminars, and programmes that depended on many people doing their part. During the pandemic, I founded Ruang Virtual and worked on its partnerships, content, and communications.

I enjoyed the work behind an event as much as the event itself: contacting people, working out what a discussion should cover, and making sure it actually happened. Years later, I was still doing versions of that work through Coffee Chat on Energy Transition and NariTalk: Policy for Sustainable Development.

03 · Across borders

Taking opportunities further

My studies took me from Indonesia to Taiwan, the United Kingdom, Singapore, and Switzerland. The route to postgraduate study involved many applications; eventually I received scholarship offers in Australia, Indonesia, Singapore, and the UK. I chose the Low Tuck Kwong Foundation Scholarship to pursue my master’s at NUS.

The opportunities extended beyond university. I joined Pacific Forum’s Young Leaders programme and its delegation to the Asia-Pacific Roundtable, participated in other policy forums, and began presenting my own work in Singapore and Switzerland. I had spent years helping organise and join discussions. I was now contributing more of my own ideas to them.

04 · Work in public

Making the work useful

Some of the work I care about most is made for people who may never read an academic paper. I built Peta Banjir Indonesia so that researchers, officials, and communities could explore long-term flood patterns at the district level. My Care Sovereignty Protocol began with a different, less visible problem: the long-term financial costs borne by unpaid caregivers. I presented that proposal at St. Gallen, where it placed second in the Global Essay Competition and was subsequently recognised by NUS.

These projects are different in form, but both reflect a question I keep returning to: once we understand a problem, how can we make that knowledge useful to someone else?
